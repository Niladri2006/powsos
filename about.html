About PawSOS

Bridging the Gap in Animal Emergency Care

When someone encounters an animal in distress on the road, valuable time is often lost trying to find working contact numbers. PawSOS coordinates rapid community reporting with local volunteers.

How PawSOS Operates

PawSOS is an open community response network. We do not replace veterinary hospitals or official government ambulances. Instead, we provide the digital dispatch layer that connects a person on the street with local volunteers who have transport, first aid, and shelter connections.

By sharing precise coordinates and photos, responders know exactly what supplies to bring before arriving on the scene.

Animal Emergency First-Aid Handbook

Essential immediate steps to stabilize a distressed animal before medical transport arrives.

🚗

Vehicular Impact / Trauma

  • Approach cautiously: Pain can cause an otherwise gentle dog to snap or bite. Place a clean towel or jacket over the eyes to reduce panic.
  • Prevent spinal damage: Do not drag or bend the animal. Slide a firm cardboard sheet or folded thick blanket underneath to act as a stretcher.
  • Severe bleeding: Apply firm, direct pressure with clean cotton or cloth for at least 4 minutes without lifting.
  • No forced liquids: An animal in shock can easily choke on oral water.
☀️

Heat Exhaustion & Dehydration

  • Warning signs: Heavy continuous panting, bright red or dry gums, glazed eyes, inability to stand up.
  • Move to shade: Relocate the animal to shaded pavement or under a tree immediately.
  • Gradual cooling: Wet paws, groin, and neck with normal room-temperature water. Never use ice-cold water, as it causes blood vessels to constrict and traps heat inside the core.
  • Safe hydration: Offer small amounts of water or oral electrolyte solution only if the animal is conscious and alert.
🕊️

Birds Tangled in String (Manja)

  • Cover immediately: Covering a bird's head with a light cotton cloth stops frantic wing-flapping within seconds.
  • Do not pull tight strings: Pulling glass-coated thread can sever tendons. Carefully snip the string with scissors without pulling against the skin.
  • Transport box: Place the bird in a ventilated cardboard box lined with newspaper in a quiet, dark room until assistance arrives.
  • Never drop water in beak: Birds have open airway slits at the tongue base; drops will enter lungs.
